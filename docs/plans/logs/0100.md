| FSR 2 input | internal | depth of the jittered hit; motion exactly 0 at a still camera; reactive 0 on a still shadow edge; the composition mask always 0 | `shaders/fsr2/input.wgsl` 120 to 150 |
| FSR 2 accumulate | display | told each input pixel is a sample at `p + 0.5 - jitter` and weighs it by that position | `fsr2/prepare.rs` 101 to 110; `shaders/fsr2/accumulate.wgsl` 103 to 120 |

- **"Half rate" is half of the internal resolution on each edge**, not of the display: a quarter
  of the pixels FSR 2 is fed. The grid does not move; the world points it samples do, by up to
  half an internal pixel (a quarter of a lighting pixel, three quarters of a display pixel).
- **The first stage that turns the world-fixed edge into a changing one is the temporal pass's
  read at the jittered hit of a quarter-rate grid** (the hit pass's jitter alone is what FSR 2
  asks for). A lighting pixel within half an internal pixel of the edge flips between lit and
  shadowed with the phase. The filter then spreads each flip over its neighbours and the
  composite's upsample over the three internal pixels in four that hold no sample of their own.
  FSR 2 receives an edge that jumps by up to a lighting pixel (3 display pixels) between phases
  while its constants say each pixel moved by the jitter alone, with motion 0 and no mask: it
  accumulates a signal that does not converge.
- **`calm,native` reads 0.00 because nothing in the frame depends on the frame index without the
  jitter**; the half-rate grid and the filter are both in that arm. It is not a level a jittered
  pipeline is known to reach: `apps/flythrough/tests/gpu_fsr2.rs`'s shadow-wobble test (Plan
  0102) reads 4.5 levels of range at a lamp shadow's boundary under the jitter and 0.000
  without, through the same half-rate grid and filter (the lamp's shadow ray starts at the
  jittered hit). The mechanism is the same one; Plan 0041's risks named the lever in 2026-10
  ("a shadow ray from the pixel's centre rather than the jittered hit").

### 2. Why `face` moved 0.10 % of the display

- The face-centre value is what the lighting pixel shows: `temporal.wgsl` 1118 reads it, 1147
  to 1151 show it with no history between, nothing later replaces it.
- It changed little because **the horizon was already fixed in the world under `hit`**:
  `column_key_visibility` (`pair.wgsl` 93 to 154) is a function of the position alone, and its
  ramp is the sun's disc wide (2 tan r, about 0.009 in the tangent), a step for this purpose.
  `face` moves that step to the nearest face boundary, at most half a voxel. The plan's premise
  ("evaluated at a hit that moves with the jitter") named the function; the fault is the
  sampling: a step sampled at a moving point of a quarter-rate grid flips as often whether it
  lies inside a face or on its boundary.
- `face-smooth` helped where it did because a ramp one voxel wide is a smaller change a jitter
  step, where a voxel covers more than a lighting pixel; hence its "key" share rising (small
  moves now register over the rig's 0.05) and its "none" share falling.
- Light history on closed 57 to 68 % in Plan 0098 Phase 9 by the same reading: the direct part's
  mean over the phases makes the lighting grid's key term nearly still, and from age 4 the
  filter takes the pixel's own variance instead of the 7 x 7's.

### 3. What the bar measures

- Frames: 33 consecutive frames at a still camera after 600 settle frames, the exposure pinned,
  the queue stopped; 32 consecutive pairs, 1.8 of the jitter's 18-phase cycles
  (`apps/flythrough/tests/gpu_wobble/terraces.rs` 317 to 357).
- The class: a display pixel is mapped to one lighting pixel by integer scaling
  (`apps/flythrough/tests/support/defects.rs` 181 to 185); that lighting pixel is in the class if
  the key light falls on its face and the lit pixels of its 3 x 3 hold key values on both sides
  of 0.5 in the pair's first frame (`terraces.rs` 164 to 199). So the class is read from the
  arm's own jittered key values, it changes every frame, and its width is 3 lighting pixels
  whatever the grid (9 display pixels at half rate, 4.5 under `full`, 3 under `native`): shares
  of arms on different grids are not shares of the same pixels.
- A moved pixel's term (`terraces.rs` 227 to 238) is read at that one lighting pixel from the
  pair and the accumulated direct part, **both written by the temporal pass, before the filter
  and the upsample**. "None" therefore means "nothing moved at this lighting pixel before the
  filter", and holds four things the rig cannot tell apart: the filter's taps (a neighbour
  flipped), the upsample's other three taps and its weights, the internal pixel's own jittered
  hit (a tread one frame, a riser the next: albedo and cosine), and FSR 2's resolve. Every class
  pixel has a neighbour on the other side of the edge by the class's definition, so the first
  two are always in reach. The 60 to 76 % is not a measure of FSR 2.
- The bar "half the distance to `calm,native` in every bin at both poses" judges W3's w 0 bin
  on about 3 moved pixels a pair (0.27 % of 1023): `face` read -33 % there on one pixel's
  difference.

### 4. What FSR 2 is given

- Motion 0 at every still pixel (`input.wgsl` 120, 123 to 130); reactive only at an edit box, a
  changed or fresh light and a translucent layer (131 to 147), none of which a still shadow edge
  is; the composition mask 0 (150); the pre-exposure the frame's exposure, constant once pinned
  (`prepare.rs` 89, 109 to 110); depth of the jittered hit, which is what it expects.
- Nothing we feed it says the edge is new content, and nothing should: a mask would trade the
  wobble for an unaccumulated, aliased edge. A one-voxel lit dash can pass its thin-feature
  test (`shaders/fsr2/lock.wgsl` 22) and be locked, which protects whatever history it has.
  The lever is the colour input.

### 1. The horizon as built

`column_key_visibility` (`crates/svet-render/src/shaders/pair.wgsl:93-154`), called once a
lighting pixel from `temporal.wgsl:1118` through `key_horizon_at` (`pair.wgsl:164-194`):

- **The map** (`crates/svet-render/src/gpu_world/columns.rs:21-23`): one `R32Sint` texel a
  column of 2 x 2 voxels (`COLUMN_CELL` 2), the highest opaque voxel's z among the resident
  chunks; 32 x 32 texels a chunk column, tiled by the window. A cell's height is the largest of
  its four voxel columns (`pair.wgsl:57-69`).
- **The lift** (`pair.wgsl:34`, `:98`): the point asked about is `q = p + 2.5 n`
  (`SEED_LIFT = COLUMN_CELL + 0.5`), the face's normal, not the face.
- **The own-cell rule** (`pair.wgsl:102-107`): `q` under its own cell's top reads 0.
- **The coarse march** (`pair.wgsl:46-48`, `:114-129`): one cell at each of 12 distances, a
  geometric series from 4 to 512 voxels (ratio 1.554): 4.0, 6.2, 9.7, 15.0, 23.4, 36.3, 56.4,
  87.7, 136, 212, 329, 512. The last interval is 183 voxels, 91 cells along an axis.
- **The refinement** (`pair.wgsl:49`, `:131-151`): at most 8 cells (16 voxels along an axis),
  in order from the start of the **one** interval whose far cell scored highest.
- **The ramp** (`pair.wgsl:152-153`): `clamp((tan e - tan h) / (2 tan r) + 1/2, 0, 1)`.

So a lit pixel reads at most 1 + 12 + 8 = 21 texels, and past 36 voxels the march sees at most
20 of the 256 to 362 cells its line crosses to 512 voxels (a line crosses `|ux| + |uy|` cells
a cell length; 362 on the diagonal). A canopy 30 to 60 voxels across, 100 to 170 voxels up the
sun's line (a 6 to 10 m tree at the 15:00 sun's 30 degrees), lies in the intervals 87.7 to
136 and 136 to 212: it is hit only if one of two samples or the 16-voxel refinement lands on
it. That is W1's class "leaves at t 64 on", 547 of the 1 054 breaks.

### 2. What the constants were set by, and what they protect

- **12 distances, 8 cells: guesses, never read.** Plan 0095 Phase 4 wrote "`COLUMN_KEY_STEPS`
  (12, a guess)" (its line 218; ADR-0079 line 151) and built it so (`logs/0095.md`, "Phase 4
  (amended plan)"). The horizon was then the **far's** instant estimate, read at the far
  normal's weight 1 only, where a pixel holds several voxels and ADR-0079's Outcome counted it
  on 1.8 % of the far's key samples. Plan 0098 (Phase 4's amendment, Phase 13) made it the key
  shadow at every distance. No reading set the count, no wobble or acne was fixed by it, and
  no pass time was taken for it apart. What the tests pin is the box form (a cell's top stands
  from its entry distance; bilinear put a 10-voxel slab's ramp at 7.9 voxels against 8.9) and
  the own-cell rule (a zenith sun lit a floor under a roof without it). A walk over every cell
  keeps both.
- **The lift, 2.5 voxels: "the top of the stated range; not tuned, the bars met on it"**
  (`logs/0086.md`, "Call 1 (2026-11-19 amendment)"). Plan 0086's call 1 gives the reason: "a
  half-voxel lift can land in the wall's own 2-voxel cell and read the wall's height as the
  air's top". With the own-cell rule that is a 0: a wall face whose air-side point shares the
  wall's map cell reads shadow, every second voxel column by the cell's parity. The same holds
  on a floor: a face one or two voxels under a cell-mate (every step of a terrace) lies under
  its own cell's top unless lifted over it. So the lift protects walls and stepped slopes from
  dark stripes a voxel wide at the map's parity, at any sun. Plan 0095 took it for the key
  horizon unchanged (its line 249; its slab test pins the ramp at `(10 - 2.5) / tan 40`).
- **What the lift costs, by arithmetic.** The horizon answers for a ray parallel to the
  face's and 2.5 voxels along the normal from it. On a floor a caster of height `H` over the
  face throws a shadow `2.5 / tan e` voxels short (2.5 at 45 degrees, 4.3 at 30), and a caster
  2.5 voxels high or less throws none: a one- or two-voxel step never shadows the tread below
  it. In angle the error is `2.5 / t`: over the disc's whole ramp (`2 tan 3` = 0.105) out to
  24 voxels. That is the slot's "under the lift" 96 faces and most of its "2.5 to 64" 50, and
  W1's 362.
- **A smaller lift brings two things back.** The parity stripes, unless the map's cell is a
  voxel (4 times the map); and the shadows of one- and two-voxel steps, which are true and are
  the hard one-voxel edges Plan 0098's W3 and this plan's Phases 1 and 1b read wobbling under
  the jitter with no arm that calms them. So the lift is not shortened in the shader on a
  code reading; step 1 of Phase 5b reads lifts of 1.5 and 0.5 on the CPU pass so the stripes
  are a count, not an argument.

### 3. What a whole walk costs

- **Every cell to 512 voxels:** 256 to 362 texel reads a lit pixel that nothing shadows,
  against 21: 12 to 17 times. A pixel a top shadows whole ends at that top (`tan h >= tan e +
  tan r`), as any shadow ray does. On the RENOIR at Portable, Phase 1 read `face-smooth`
  (three more marches a lit pixel) at +0.57 to +1.45 ms on the temporal pass, so one march is
  about 0.19 to 0.48 ms there; every cell on open lit ground is then +2.3 to +8 ms (**a
  guess**, linear in the reads), beside a temporal pass of 3.04 to 3.21 ms that is already
  over the Denoise line's 2.0 ms on that adapter. Not a default on any profile.
- **An early out by height:** no top matters past the distance where the disc's lower edge
  clears the window's highest top, `q.z + t (tan e - tan r) > top_max`: with tops 100 voxels
  over the point, 196 voxels at the 15:00 sun and 111 at noon (arithmetic). It needs one
  number a frame, or the coarse level below.
- **Two levels, the same answer.** A second, coarse map holds the largest top of each 8 x 8
  cells (16 voxels; 4 x 4 a chunk column, 1/64 of the map's texels). The walk steps the coarse
  cells (32 to 45 to 512 voxels) and enters one's cells only where `(top_max - q.z) /
  t_entry` exceeds the larger of the running `tan h` and `tan e - tan r`: a bound, since
  every cell inside is entered later and is no higher, so the visibility equals the every-cell
  walk's. On open lit ground it enters few: about 40 to 80 reads, two to four marches, +0.2
  to +1.4 ms on the RENOIR at Portable (**a guess**). A third level (a chunk column's top,
  64 voxels, 8 to 12 steps to 512) would bring the lit case to about today's 21 reads (a
  guess). The CPU pass counts the reads a face for each form before any shader is written.
- **Stepping at the cell size with the early outs** is the every-cell walk; the levels are
  what makes it affordable. A fixed finer geometric series (24, 48 distances) stays a sampling
  and keeps the fault at a smaller size, so it is not an arm.

### 2. What on a sunlit face can change from frame to frame, and why a shadowed face has none of it

The key light's term at a lighting pixel is `key_light_at(rec.p, rec.n) * k`
(`temporal.wgsl`, the last lines of the key block; `key_light_of` in `sky.wgsl` is the sun's
illuminance times `max(n.l, 0)`). Its two factors, as the defaults run them:

| factor | what it is under the defaults | where | its shape across a surface of small faces |
|---|---|---|---|
| the cosine | `max(n.l, 0)` with `rec.n`, the shading normal: the face's own normal at the far normal's weight 0, mixed toward the 27-count gradient normal across the band | `surface_normal`, `far_normal_weight` (`walk.wgsl`) | near, a step at every edge between two faces of different direction (a tread at 0.707 beside a riser at 0 under the noon sun) |
| the visibility `k` | the column horizon at the hit lifted 2.5 voxels along the face's normal: 0 under its own cell's top, else a ramp the sun's disc wide | `key_horizon_at`, `column_key_visibility` (`key_horizon.wgsl`); `lift_n` is the face normal (`temporal.wgsl`) | binary but for the ramp; two faces of one step read it at points 3.5 voxels apart (2.5 up for the tread, 2.5 out for the riser), in different map cells, so their answers are independent; under `march` the 12 sampled cells also change with the hit's place inside one face |

Nothing else in the default light is like this. The sky's term is a baked visibility read
through a blend of cells and, across the band, by the shading normal
(`store_value_by_normal`); it changes between face directions too, but it is the sky's
irradiance, not the sun's. No list light shines outdoors. **On a face the sun does not reach,
`k` is 0 at every sample and the cosine multiplies nothing: there is no edge of the sun's term
for any stage below to place wrongly.** That is the owner's "only on sunned parts", and it is
established. How much smaller the sky term's own steps are is a ratio of two irradiances the
code does not fix: a reading (`keyoff`, below).

The stages that place those edges, in order:

1. **The hit** (`hit.wgsl`, `pixel_ray` in `common.wgsl`): one ray an internal pixel, through
   the pixel's centre moved by FSR 2's jitter. At rest the jitter moves the hit; in motion the
   camera does. Either can put it on the next face.
2. **The lighting grid** (`downsample` in `composite.wgsl`, `full_px` in `common.wgsl`):
   lighting pixel `lp` copies the hit of internal pixel `2 lp + offset`, one of each 2 x 2. The
   whole sun term is evaluated there and nowhere else (`shade.wgsl` writes no key light under
   the defaults: `key_from_store`; the temporal pass reads the horizon).
3. **The a-trous filter** (`atrous.wgsl`): with no light history every pixel has age 0, so the
   variance is the 7 x 7's and a lit and shadowed mix reads as noise to be spread (the reading
   after Phase 1's stop, section 1).
4. **The upsample** (`lit_at` in `composite.wgsl`): each internal pixel takes the four lighting
   pixels around it by bilinear weight times `max(n.n', 0)^32` (the face normals under `weights
   face`, `filter_normal` in `hitrec.wgsl`) times a plane term. Across an edge between two
   directions the weights vanish, which keeps a riser from taking a tread's sun, **unless all
   four vanish: then it takes the nearest tap's light whatever its face** (`nearest`). Inside
   one plane (a shadow's edge on a tread) the weights are plain bilinear, so the edge is a ramp
   two internal pixels wide whose place is set by which lighting pixels' hits fell on which
   side.
5. **FSR 2** (`fsr2/`, not re-read here): given an internal image whose sun edges sit where
   stages 2 to 4 put them this frame.

So per pixel, between two frames, on a sunlit surface: (i) the lighting pixel's hit lands on
another face, and both factors change at once, by up to the whole sun term; (ii) it stays on
its face and crosses a `k` edge inside it; (iii) nothing changes at its own lighting pixel but
a neighbour's did, and the filter or the upsample carries it over; (iv) the upsample's four
weights vanish this frame and not the last, and a face shows a neighbour's light. On a
shadowed surface (i) to (iv) all happen and change nothing the sun term can show.
