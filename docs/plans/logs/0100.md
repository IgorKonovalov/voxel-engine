| a-trous | lighting | the direct part, the key term in it, is filtered: a pixel of age under 4 takes its variance from the 7 x 7 pixels around it, so a hard shadow edge reads as noise (sigma is 4 standard deviations of a lit and shadowed mix) and four 5 x 5 iterations at strides 1, 2, 4, 8 pull light across it, by weights computed from this frame's jittered samples | `shaders/atrous.wgsl` 247, 255 to 278, 330, 356 |
| composite | internal | each internal pixel takes the four lighting pixels around it, bilinear times normal and plane weights against its own jittered hit; only the one pixel in four that the grid copied reads light evaluated at its own hit (`f` is 0 there) | `shaders/composite.wgsl` 132 to 176, 340 |
| FSR 2 input | internal | depth of the jittered hit; motion exactly 0 at a still camera; reactive 0 on a still shadow edge; the composition mask always 0 | `shaders/fsr2/input.wgsl` 120 to 150 |
| FSR 2 accumulate | display | told each input pixel is a sample at `p + 0.5 - jitter` and weighs it by that position | `fsr2/prepare.rs` 101 to 110; `shaders/fsr2/accumulate.wgsl` 103 to 120 |

- **"Half rate" is half of the internal resolution on each edge**, not of the display: a quarter
  of the pixels FSR 2 is fed. The grid does not move; the world points it samples do, by up to
  half an internal pixel (a quarter of a lighting pixel, three quarters of a display pixel).
- **The first stage that turns the world-fixed edge into a changing one is the temporal pass's
  read at the jittered hit of a quarter-rate grid** (the hit pass's jitter alone is what FSR 2
  asks for). A lighting pixel within half an internal pixel of the edge flips between lit and
  shadowed with the phase. The filter then spreads each flip over its neighbours and the
  composite's upsample over the three internal pixels in four that hold no sample of their own.
  FSR 2 receives an edge that jumps by up to a lighting pixel (3 display pixels) between phases
  while its constants say each pixel moved by the jitter alone, with motion 0 and no mask: it
  accumulates a signal that does not converge.
- **`calm,native` reads 0.00 because nothing in the frame depends on the frame index without the
  jitter**; the half-rate grid and the filter are both in that arm. It is not a level a jittered
  pipeline is known to reach: `apps/flythrough/tests/gpu_fsr2.rs`'s shadow-wobble test (Plan
  0102) reads 4.5 levels of range at a lamp shadow's boundary under the jitter and 0.000
  without, through the same half-rate grid and filter (the lamp's shadow ray starts at the
  jittered hit). The mechanism is the same one; Plan 0041's risks named the lever in 2026-10
  ("a shadow ray from the pixel's centre rather than the jittered hit").

### 2. Why `face` moved 0.10 % of the display

- The face-centre value is what the lighting pixel shows: `temporal.wgsl` 1118 reads it, 1147
  to 1151 show it with no history between, nothing later replaces it.
- It changed little because **the horizon was already fixed in the world under `hit`**:
  `column_key_visibility` (`pair.wgsl` 93 to 154) is a function of the position alone, and its
  ramp is the sun's disc wide (2 tan r, about 0.009 in the tangent), a step for this purpose.
  `face` moves that step to the nearest face boundary, at most half a voxel. The plan's premise
  ("evaluated at a hit that moves with the jitter") named the function; the fault is the
  sampling: a step sampled at a moving point of a quarter-rate grid flips as often whether it
  lies inside a face or on its boundary.
- `face-smooth` helped where it did because a ramp one voxel wide is a smaller change a jitter
  step, where a voxel covers more than a lighting pixel; hence its "key" share rising (small
  moves now register over the rig's 0.05) and its "none" share falling.
- Light history on closed 57 to 68 % in Plan 0098 Phase 9 by the same reading: the direct part's
  mean over the phases makes the lighting grid's key term nearly still, and from age 4 the
  filter takes the pixel's own variance instead of the 7 x 7's.

### 3. What the bar measures

- Frames: 33 consecutive frames at a still camera after 600 settle frames, the exposure pinned,
  the queue stopped; 32 consecutive pairs, 1.8 of the jitter's 18-phase cycles
  (`apps/flythrough/tests/gpu_wobble/terraces.rs` 317 to 357).
- The class: a display pixel is mapped to one lighting pixel by integer scaling
  (`apps/flythrough/tests/support/defects.rs` 181 to 185); that lighting pixel is in the class if
  the key light falls on its face and the lit pixels of its 3 x 3 hold key values on both sides
  of 0.5 in the pair's first frame (`terraces.rs` 164 to 199). So the class is read from the
  arm's own jittered key values, it changes every frame, and its width is 3 lighting pixels
  whatever the grid (9 display pixels at half rate, 4.5 under `full`, 3 under `native`): shares
  of arms on different grids are not shares of the same pixels.
- A moved pixel's term (`terraces.rs` 227 to 238) is read at that one lighting pixel from the
  pair and the accumulated direct part, **both written by the temporal pass, before the filter
  and the upsample**. "None" therefore means "nothing moved at this lighting pixel before the
  filter", and holds four things the rig cannot tell apart: the filter's taps (a neighbour
  flipped), the upsample's other three taps and its weights, the internal pixel's own jittered
  hit (a tread one frame, a riser the next: albedo and cosine), and FSR 2's resolve. Every class
  pixel has a neighbour on the other side of the edge by the class's definition, so the first
  two are always in reach. The 60 to 76 % is not a measure of FSR 2.
- The bar "half the distance to `calm,native` in every bin at both poses" judges W3's w 0 bin
  on about 3 moved pixels a pair (0.27 % of 1023): `face` read -33 % there on one pixel's
  difference.

### 4. What FSR 2 is given

- Motion 0 at every still pixel (`input.wgsl` 120, 123 to 130); reactive only at an edit box, a
  changed or fresh light and a translucent layer (131 to 147), none of which a still shadow edge
  is; the composition mask 0 (150); the pre-exposure the frame's exposure, constant once pinned
  (`prepare.rs` 89, 109 to 110); depth of the jittered hit, which is what it expects.
- Nothing we feed it says the edge is new content, and nothing should: a mask would trade the
  wobble for an unaccumulated, aliased edge. A one-voxel lit dash can pass its thin-feature
  test (`shaders/fsr2/lock.wgsl` 22) and be locked, which protects whatever history it has.
  The lever is the colour input.

- **The plan's "2.25 times the pixels of the light pass" is wrong by the code**: 2.25 is the
  display over the internal resolution. The internal grid holds 4 times the lighting grid's
  pixels (`frame/targets.rs` 117, scale 2), and the horizon is the temporal pass's, not the
  light pass's.
- **Recommended: A, with B if its reading earns it.** A is the stateless form of what light
  history did for this fault (a lighting grid whose key term does not change at rest), it is
  ADR-0076's rule read literally, and it costs nothing. What it gives up: the shadow edge is no
  longer supersampled by the jitter, so it is as sharp as the lighting grid (a ramp of up to 3
  display pixels, stepped), as `native`'s is at 2. Where the centre ray would have met another
  face (a step's silhouette) the point lies on the hit face's plane beyond its edge; the horizon
  is defined there and holds still, and the lighting pixel still changes when its jittered hit
  changes plane, which is geometry and FSR 2's to resolve.
- C is the correct jittered input and the costly one; it is built only on the owner's word
  (the plan's question 6), and only if its ceiling reading says it would work.
