## Phase 1: The reference bake, and the count it needs

### The layout (done-when 7's "dev states")

- **Pool** (`bake::Store`, one storage buffer, `Lighting::bake_pool_bytes` 256 MiB clamped to the device's
  storage binding and buffer size; `--light table` allocates 64 B and `frame_bytes` counts the pool only under
  `Light::Baked`). Words: 0 the frame's `store_missing` (cleared each encode), 4..7 the mirror window's origin in
  chunks, 8..11 its size, from 16 a word per window chunk naming its block's first word (0 none). A block: 512
  directory words (`run offset from the block << 11 | faces`, 0 no surface), then two words a cell: `rgb9e5`
  (exponent bias 15 in the top five bits, red low), then `key 12 | flags 4 | dominant material 16`, key
  `voxel in brick << 3 | face`, sorted.
- **Bindings.** The read binds the pool at the temporal pass's group 2 binding 12 (its stage then holds 11 storage
  buffers, the limit `STORAGE_BUFFERS_NEEDED` asks). The light pass binds nothing new: under `Light::Baked`
  (`Params.cache` bit 2) a cube face draws no emissive sample and casts no emissive shadow ray. The bake is its own
  pipeline (march.wgsl + light.wgsl + bake.wgsl, group 3: a 16 B job uniform and the working buffer) with the
  overrides `RAND_HASHED` (`rand` hashes `(stream, sample index, dim)`, no blue noise, no frame) and
  `EMISSION_UNIT` (no emission scale).
- **Working buffer** (`BakeFace`, 80 B a face, `BAKE_BATCH_FACES` 65 536 faces, 5.2 MB): in, the voxel, face, first
  sample index and the round's samples; out, the round's sums unclamped and clamped (rgb, f32, one invocation adds
  its face's samples in index order), the round's luminance second moment, largest luminance and its emitter's
  material, shadow rays and their summed length. The running sums are kept on the CPU in f64; the stop is decided
  there after each round, and the cell packed there. A submit holds at most `SUBMIT_SAMPLES` 10 M samples
  (9 765 faces at `BAKE_ROUND`) and is waited on (its readback).
- **Candidate table per job**: the chunks holding a light-sample range whose box lies within `light_radius x 64`
  voxels of the job chunk's box, nearest 1024, coordinate order, weight `power / max(gap^2, 64^2)`; written where
  the frame's table goes, before the job's submits. `GpuWorld::hold_lights(r)` holds ranges out to the bake's reach
  (extent corner + light radius + 1) and `bake_until` syncs until `lights_settled`.
- **Window** `(1 - (d / L)^4)^2`, d from the face point to the kept emitter point; at `q >= 1` no shadow ray.
- **SE** (`bake::se_levels`): the mean luminance's standard error `sqrt((m2/n - mean^2)/n)` through
  ACES + 2.2 at exposure 8, half the level difference between `mean + se` and `mean - se`.

## Phase 1a: The stop's submits

### The layout (done-when's "dev states")

- **Across jobs: tables in one buffer, indexed from the face.** A slot per open job (`OPEN_JOBS` 256) in the
  bake's group 3 binding 2 (`tables`, 1026 16-byte words a slot: the job's `job_table` header and entries, then
  its chunk), written once when the job opens. Each entry of the working buffer names its slot (`BakeFace.slot`,
  the old pad word 17). The bake's module is march.wgsl + light.wgsl + bake.wgsl with two lines replaced in
  `bake.rs` (`bake_source`, each asserted to occur once): light.wgsl's `table_slot` reads `bake_table_slot(i)`,
  and march.wgsl's `lod_level` takes `bake_origin_chunk()`, the job's chunk (Phase 1 set every view's origin to
  the job chunk's centre, whose chunk is the same). One dispatch a submit. The frame parameters are written once
  a bake.
- **Rounds a submit.** An entry is one round of one face (`first`, `samples`). A face takes, in one submit, the
  rounds that cannot end it (to `n_min`), else one; jobs are opened while the submit has room; then, once no job
  can open, further rounds a layer at a time, each face up to the rounds its standard error expects
  (`n (se / BAKE_SE)^2`, at most `n_max`). The CPU adds each face's rounds in order and reads the stop after each;
  a round past the stop is counted in `BakeReport::wasted`, not in `samples`, rays or the sums. `k` is therefore
  per face and per submit: `BakeReport::max_rounds_in_submit` reads it (92 at the vein).
- **Caps kept:** `SUBMIT_SAMPLES` 10 M (9 765 entries at `BAKE_ROUND`), `BAKE_BATCH_FACES` 65 536 entries. Blocks
  are placed in the jobs' order when listed, whatever order the jobs end in, so the pool's layout is Phase 1's.
- Test knobs: `BakeSettings::submit_rounds` (1 is one round a submit) and `jobs_alone` (one job a submit).

## Phase 4: The samples cut, the shared seeds and the filter (stopped at its stop rule)

### The layout (dev states)

- **The seed forms** (`bake::SeedForm`, `BakeSettings::form`). The independent form's module is the reference's,
  byte for byte (`bake_source_for(Independent) == bake_source()`, asserted), so `source_hash()` and the three
  reference files stand. Each shared form's module replaces bake.wgsl's stream line once and appends
  `shaders/bake_seeds.wgsl`: **chunk-side** keys the stream by the chunk and the side (`bake_side_key(chunk,
  face)`), **global-side** by the side alone, **lattice** by the side and rotates every random dimension of a face
  by its point of the R2 Kronecker lattice of its two plane coordinates plus the dimension times 0.618 (a
  Cranley-Patterson rotation; the plan's "offset by a low-discrepancy step" read this way). One pipeline a form,
  built on its first bake.
- **The filter** (`bake::tent`, `BakeSettings::tent`): the table's blend kernel (`cache_blend.wgsl`), 3 x 3, run on
  the CPU after the bake from the packed cells (the store keeps the raw words); a dig re-filters its ring's chunks
  and the 26 around each. Faces of a cut share one count, so the blend's count weights cancel.
- **The store file** of a cut: magic `VXEBAKE2`, the key plus the seed form and the filter, and under the filter
  the raw blocks after the filtered ones. The reference's `VXEBAKE1` files are unchanged.
- **The swap** (fly-through): `--cut FORM:N[:off],...` under `--reference` bakes or loads each cut beside the
  reference's file (`vein.bin` gives `vein.chunk-side-512.bin`); `F` cycles the reference, each cut and the table;
  `Frame::swap_store_image` writes the selected store's blocks into the pool at the next frame and holds the other;
  a dig re-bakes the store in the pool and queues its boxes for each held store, re-baked when swapped in. The
  banner and HUD row: `reference: light store <name> (F cycles reference, <cuts>, table)`.
- `BakeSettings::timer` times each submit's dispatch on the GPU's timestamps (`BakeReport::dispatch_seconds`).

## Phase 5a: Glass as a filter (stopped before code, 2026-11-08, at 3ac2832)

No code written, no GPU run, no re-bake, no binary. The tree read at 3ac2832 (sources as fdbe8d3):

- **The layer target holds one transmittance.** `layer_out` (march.wgsl group 1 binding 2; `marcher.rs`,
  `tex("layer", Rgba16Float)`) is premultiplied rgb plus a scalar transmittance, and composite.wgsl applies it as
  `c = layer.xyz * ex + layer.w * c` (FSR 2's variant compiles the same file). ADR-0047's `T(L) = tint^(L / d)` is per
  channel, so `T(L) * behind` needs three values beside the reflected rgb: a wider or second target (`marcher.rs`),
  or `L` plus the row's tint recomputed in the composite, and composite.wgsl changes in any case. Neither file is in
  the phase's Files touched.
- **The store is bound to the temporal pass only** (temporal.wgsl group 2 binding 12, `read_write`, atomic; its
  `store_value(v, face)` is declared there). The hit pass, which walks the glass, has no store binding.
- **The lamp's bounce is a world-space probe volume** (probe_gather.wgsl, group 3 of the probe passes,
  `probe_irradiance(x, n)`), gathered on the lighting grid at each pixel's own hit. For a glass pixel that hit is the
  opaque one behind; the glass face's bounce needs the probe group bound where `face_light` is computed.
- **The lamp on the glass face:** the opaque path's lamp term is the light pass's shadowed sample through the a-trous
  filter at the pixel's hit; the glass face has no pixel of its own, so its lamp term would be analytic
  (`lamp(p)` times `n . l`, as `translucent_march` has it now without `n . l`), with or without a shadow ray.
- **`face_light`'s units** are not fixed by the ADR: an opaque face displays `albedo * E / PI`; `R * face_light` reads
  either `R * E / PI` (untinted reflection) or `R * albedo * E / PI`. Done-when 4's third assertion ("`R * face_light`
  from its store cell, within 10 %") reads differently under each.
- **What is behind a pane is a fallback today:** `cacheable(rec)` (march.wgsl) is false for a hit with translucent
  layers in front, so the opaque hit behind glass never reads its store cell (the parked "A held fallback still moves
  on screen" sits on this path; left alone).
- **The bake side** (in Files touched): `list_chunk_faces` lists an opaque voxel's faces toward a voxel that is not
  opaque (`opaque_table`: not air, not translucent). Glass faces would join the brick's sorted face run, so a chunk
  holding glass gets a different block layout, while its opaque faces' cells can stay the same. Done-when 6's
  "opaque blocks bit-identical" reads exactly on chunks with no translucent voxel; chunks with glass (the hall's castle
  glass, any placed pane) would be compared cell by cell on the opaque faces' keys.

## Phase 6: The reach cut, the brick level and the rings (exploration mode, the amendment of 2026-11-08)

### The layout (dev states)

- **The brick record.** Under a ring store every block carries, just before each surface brick's run, `BRICK_WORDS` 7
  words (28 B, not the plan's 24 B): six direction values in rgb9e5 (face order), then `material | direction mask << 16`.
  The directory word still points at the brick's cells (`run << 11 | cells`), so `march.wgsl`'s glass read and every
  plain block are unchanged; a brick at the brick level has a run of 0 cells. The store header's word 12
  (`STORE_FLAGS`, bit 0) says the blocks carry records; word 1 counts this frame's pixels that read one.
- **The values.** A direction's value is the mean of the brick's opaque faces of that direction: a face ring's baked
  means; at the brick level, `BRICK_LEVEL_SAMPLES` 64 a direction spread over its opaque faces, `ceil(64 / count)` each,
  drawn through the same job loop (each face carries its own `n_min` / `n_max`). The material is the face sample's
  with the largest luminance over the brick. A brick over `BRICK_FACES_MAX` 512 faces holds no cells in a face ring.
  Glass faces at the brick level are not sampled and read no value.
- **The read** (`temporal.wgsl`): a face with no cell, in a store with records, reads `store_brick_value`: the 2 x 2
  bricks around the voxel's centre in the face's plane, at its own brick's layer along the normal, bilinear by the
  centre's place between brick centres, over those holding the direction, each times its own material's scale; its
  own brick's alone where none has weight. `bake.wgsl` and `march.wgsl` are unchanged, so the source hash and every
  existing store file stand.
- **The rings** (`bake::Rings`, `BakeSettings::rings`): Chebyshev distance in chunks from the pose's chunk; within 1
  face cells at 512, within 2 at 128, within 4 at 32, within 12 the brick level; independent seeds, the filter on. The
  store covers the brick ring clipped to the world (the whole level, 3168 surface chunks; the showcase's 445). A dig on
  a ring store re-bakes its ring's bricks at their chunk's tier and keeps the records of the bricks outside it.
- **Under the key:** `--cut rings:B/R32/R128/R512` and `--cut bricks:B` (the brick level everywhere out to `B`, added so
  the owner can see the brick level up close); the name (`rings 12/4/2/1`, `bricks 12`) in the banner's F row; the
  HUD's store row adds `brick level N`. Files: `<pose>.rings-12-4-2-1.bin`, `<pose>.bricks-12.bin`, magic `VXEBAKE3`
  with the rings in the key. The bake's done line now also prints its submit-to-readback seconds and submits.

### The layout (dev states)

- **The axes.** `bake::Rings` gains `cell` (8 or 4 voxels) and `brick_samples` (samples a brick direction). The
  fly-through takes `--cut bricks:B/CELL/SAMPLES` (`bricks:B` stays 8/64); the banner and HUD name each

  "brick level's cell" only where the cell is not 8, so the Phase 6 ring files keep their key.
- **Samples are a brick direction's**, as the phase's arithmetic counts them: `ceil(samples / faces of that direction
  in the brick)` a face, whatever the cell. At cell 4 a 4-voxel cell's direction draws in proportion to its faces, so
  4/64 has about 8 samples a cell direction, and 4/512 about as many a cell as 8/64 a brick.
- **The record at cell 4 (a choice the phase leaves open):** eight 7-word cell records in cell order `x + 2 y + 4 z`
  (56 words before each brick's run), each with its own six direction values, its own material (the largest
  luminance sample in that cell) and its own direction mask. Cell 8 is the Phase 6 record unchanged. The store's flags
  word gains bit 1 (`FLAG_CELL4`).

  in the next brick (or chunk) is read the same way as one in the same brick, so the bilinear crosses a brick border as
  it crosses a cell border. Cells without the direction drop out of the weights as before.

## Phase 8: The queue at 8/4096 (exploration mode; as re-read by the amendment of 2026-11-08, b991868)

### The layout (dev states)

- **The hook (ADR-0017's, declared in `vxe-world`).** `ChunkPrep::prepare(coord, &Chunk) -> Prepared`, set by
  `Streamer::set_prep`; a worker runs it on each full chunk it loads (generated or restored), and the result rides in
  `StreamEvents::prepared` beside `loaded`. `Prepared` wraps an `Arc<dyn Any + Send + Sync>` the world never reads.
- **What the workers prepare (`bake::FacePrep`, `ChunkMasks`).** Each brick's opaque and solid masks (the palette
  decode the listing spends its time in), a uniform brick by its class; the classes they were made under ride with
  them. The listing is `list_faces_by(own masks, neighbours' masks)`; `list_chunk_faces` is the same function over
  masks decoded inline. A chunk with no prepared result, every chunk of a bounded world, and a neighbour without one
  are decoded inline. The queue drops a chunk's masks when it opens its job, when an edit box comes within a voxel of
  it, and over 64 MiB, the farthest from the camera first.
- **The job loop.** `run_jobs` became `JobLoop`, resumable: `submit` draws at most a sample cap (and at least one
  entry), opens pending jobs into free table slots and hands each ended job's block to a sink. The queue takes the
  last 16 of the 256 table slots, a one-off bake or a dig's re-bake the rest, and each step writes its open jobs'
  candidate tables again from the ranges held then.
- **The queue (`bake::BakeQueue`, `Frame::start_bake_queue` and `bake_queue_step`).** Its store is the mirror's
  window, every block with brick records, every brick at the brick level (8 voxels, `QUEUE_BRICK_SAMPLES` 4096 a
  brick direction, no filter). A step: follow the window (a move drops the blocks outside it and writes the table
  again from the CPU; a window of another size empties the store); free the blocks of chunks the mirror does not hold
  full; while the open jobs hold under two slices, sort the full chunks with no block, not known faceless and not in a
  job by squared chunk distance from the camera, ties by the cosine to the view (then coordinate), and open up to 16
  ready ones; one submit within the slice; the fades a frame on.
- **The gates.** Reach: every chunk whose box is within `light_radius` chunks of the candidate's box (the candidate
  table's metric) is not waiting for an upload, and on a streamed world is resident full and mirrored full. Lights:
  every chunk with lights within the same reach holds a current range (`GpuWorld::lights_ready_near`). The ranges are
  held out to `r_full + light_radius + 1` on the streamed world, else to the window's far corner plus the light
  radius. A candidate not ready is checked again 30 steps on; an opening that finds none ready waits 8 steps.
- **The slice.** One submit a frame, the GPU waited idle first (only when a job is open), its samples
  `bake_slice_ms` over the running ns a sample (0.8 / 0.2 mean of each submit's ms, submit to readback, over its
  samples), at least 16 384 and at most `SUBMIT_SAMPLES`. A step without a slice (the pre-roll) draws 2 000 000.
- **The rounds.** One invocation draws an entry's samples in turn, so the slices take rounds of 4 samples
  (`QUEUE_ROUND`) and a step without a slice rounds of 32 (`UNSLICED_ROUND`); readings below.
- **The fade.** A block that replaces one of the same directory takes its old place, the pool shows the old words,
  and for `FADE` 16 frames the CPU writes each record's six direction values (and any cell's value) at `frame / 16`
  of the way in rgb9e5, then the target. A first block, or one of another directory, is written directly. The queue
  opens only chunks without a block, so in this phase nothing reaches the fade: a dig keeps its in-frame re-bake,
  written directly; a job running when an edit box touches its chunk is thrown away and its place freed.
- **The fly-through.** `--light queue` on any world, and `--light baked` on the streamed world (the refusal lifted):
  no store file; the pre-roll syncs (and streams) and steps until the camera's chunk and its 26 neighbours hold their
  blocks or are known to need none, then a step a frame after the sync, whose copies are submitted first. The banner
  row `light: live queue, bricks 8/4096, slice 1.5 ms, read store (F swaps the table)`; the HUD's store row adds
  `queue depth N, jobs J, slice X ms`; stdout a `bake queue:` line a second (60 frames headless). `--bake-slice MS`;
  `--glide V` and `--pose vein-flight` (the settle rig's flight start, facing the vein pose, 180 voxels) for the flight.

## Phase 8a: The queue's throughput (exploration mode; the amendment of 2026-11-08, 0983da8)

### The layout (dev states)

- **The workgroup (`bake.wgsl`).** One workgroup of 64 lanes (`BAKE_LANES`) an entry; lane `l` draws the round's samples
  `l`, `l + 64`, ..., each with the same `(stream, first + s)` as before, so the samples and seeds are unchanged. The
  lanes' sums (rgb, clamped rgb, `lum2`, rays, ray length) reduce in workgroup memory by a tree of 6 steps; the largest
  luminance and its material reduce with ties to the lowest sample index, as one lane in turn would pick. The round's
  length is read through `workgroupUniformLoad`, so the barrier is in uniform control flow. Entries span the dispatch's x
  then y (32 768 a row), since a submit holds up to 65 536.
- **The rounds.** `QUEUE_ROUND` and `UNSLICED_ROUND` are 64 (a sample a lane), not 4 and 32. `BAKE_ROUND` (1024, the
  one-off bakes) stays.
- **The job loop.** `JobLoop::submit` became `dispatch` (plan, write, submit, copy the results and the dispatch's two
  timestamps into a pooled readback buffer, `map_async`) and `collect` (reads the submits whose map has fired, oldest
  first, waiting only until at most `unread` are left). A face carries `queued`, its samples in flight; its next round
  starts at `n + queued`, and a job closes when its faces are done and none has samples in flight. A one-off bake and a
  dig's re-bake call `submit`, which is `collect(0)`, `dispatch`, `collect(0)`: waited as before.
- **The slice.** No GPU-idle wait. A step polls the device without blocking, reads back what is mapped, and dispatches
  while fewer than `IN_FLIGHT` (2) submits are unread. The cap is `bake_slice_ms` over the running ns a sample, now from
  the loop's own timestamps (a query set of 2 resolved into the head of each submit's readback) of the submits read back
  that step, one or two frames late; without timestamps the ns stays at its first guess (20), a fixed 75 000 samples.
  The HUD's and stdout's slice ms is the timestamps' figure.
- **The pre-roll** (a step without a slice) keeps two submits in flight and waits only for the oldest, so the CPU lists
  and plans while the GPU draws; with nothing left to send it waits out what is in flight.
- **`--queue-file PATH`** (fly-through, under `--light queue`): the store written to `PATH` when the queue first empties,
  keyed by `queue_settings()`, for the hall's comparison below.
- The source hash changed. No file under `live/bake/` was re-baked: `live/session8.sh` reads none (the queue starts from
  nothing). Every store file there is now refused by its hash and would re-bake on its next use; the reference at the
  hall takes about 24 min.

## Phase 8c: Coarse first, a larger near bake (exploration mode; the amendment of 2026-11-08, 9364b50)

### The layout (dev states)

- **The coarse flag.** Bit 31 of a chunk table word (`bake::TABLE_COARSE`) marks a block that is the coarse pass; the
  rest of the word is the block's first word. The store keeps the set of coarse chunks and writes the flag at each put
  and window move. `temporal.wgsl` masks the flag in its two table reads, and counts in header word 2
  (`STORE_COARSE_READS`) the lighting pixels with a value whose voxel's chunk is coarse; the frame clears words 0 to 2.
  `StoreStats` gains `coarse_reads` and `coarse_chunks`.
- **A job's own count.** `Job::brick_samples` overrides the settings' count a brick direction. On cells of 8, such a job
  draws a direction's samples on `ceil(N / 64)` of its opaque faces (4 at 256), spread evenly in key order, so each
  entry fills its workgroup; the record is their mean (`brick_record` skips faces with no sample).
- **The queue.** Candidates are class 0 (no block) and class 1 (a coarse block), sorted by class, then squared distance,
  then the view. A class 0 chunk within `near` chunks (Chebyshev) of the camera's is baked at 4096 a brick direction,
  one beyond at `coarse_samples`; class 1 at 4096. A refinement lists the same faces, so its block has the coarse one's
  directory and fades from it over `FADE` frames (Phase 8's same-layout path). Class 1 is opened only by an opening
  that found no class 0 candidate ready. Each class checks up to 16 candidates an opening (64 in a step without a
  slice). A step without a slice lists the gated candidates across up to 16 threads (`std::thread::scope`); a sliced
  step lists in turn as before. The loop's samples left are read once an opening and counted on.
- **The pre-roll.** The chunks within `--near` of the camera's chunk (Chebyshev, 125 at 2) at 4096; then the coarse
  pass over the full chunks the mirror holds when the near ones settle; it ends when each has passed (a block, no face,
  or its reach or lights not ready at its last check), or 5 s past the near chunks (`COARSE_PRE_ROLL_MS`). A chunk not
  covered takes its coarse pass at the head of the live queue.
- **The fly-through.** `--coarse N` (0 to 4096, default 256; 0 no coarse pass, the near chunks alone before the first
  frame) and `--near R` (0 to 8, default 2). Banner row: `light: live queue, bricks 8/4096, coarse 8/256 first, near 2
  at 4096, slice 1.5 ms, read store (F swaps the table)`. HUD store row: `queue depth N, coarse C, jobs J, slice X
  ms`. Stdout: the pre-roll's line gives both parts (time, chunks, samples), the box if it ended the pass; the line a
  second adds `coarse C chunks` and `no value X coarse Y of P pixels` (Y: the share of the lighting pixels reading a
  coarse block).
