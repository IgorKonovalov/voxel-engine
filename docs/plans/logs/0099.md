**What the split counts** (`crates/svet-render/src/bake/stages.rs`): a thread-local tally of
the time in `wgpu` calls and the bytes written, read at each stage lap. `render` is every
`write_buffer` (the store's block, table, header and parent writes; the job uniform and faces;
the candidate tables with their build, `JobLoop::refresh_tables`; the parameters), the encode
from the staging buffer's pick to `map_async` with the submit, the device polls (the step's
`Poll` and the job loop's waits), the mapped read with its copy and the unmap, and the bake's
world bind group in the step's setup. `movable` is the rest of each stage. The placement's and
the summaries' moves between stages carry their render share with them. Bytes by buffer:
`pool` (blocks, shown words, sky-stale marks), `table` (table words, removals, a window move's
header and table), `summary` (parent records), `job` (job uniform, faces, candidate tables,
parameters).

## Phase 2: The queue in two halves, in lockstep

### Step 1 (`39ef163b`, wip): write lists, the fade clock, the mirror's shadow

- `bake/writes.rs`: `Write`, `WriteList`, `ListMaker` (units appended whole, an edit's list,
  refused over-cap units sent as an edit's by the store), `Lists` (the render half: whole lists
  in order under `cap`, edits first and never carried; a first block's landing word written as
  the apply frame plus one and reported), `PoolApplier`. `Store` routes its pool writes through
  `emit` while `Store::out` is set (only inside a queue step); `begin_unit`/`end_unit` group the
  writes of one landing; `Store::landed` patches the copy's landing word.
- The step (`queue/step.rs`): `step` = `step_books` (every stage as before) + `apply_lists`, a
  new 22nd stage `apply` (`STEP_STAGES`), whose `wgpu` share is now where the pool writes'

### 1. What a ring-1 job waits on (code)

- **The edit.** `take_boxes` (`bake/queue/digs.rs:24`) withdraws, in the edit's step, every job
  that is not key-only in a ring-1 chunk and **every dig job in any ring chunk** (lines 47 to
  55: `dig_open.contains_key(c) || slots.iter().any(touches)`). A job of another kind in a ring
  chunk the box does not touch is not withdrawn: it is marked stale (line 68) and its block is
  thrown away when it ends (`Placement::place`, `bake/queue/step.rs:536`). So a ring-2 job of
  the previous dig is withdrawn at the next edit if the new ring reaches its chunk.
- **The fade.** Ring 1 never waits for one: `open_digs` filters by `d.near || !fading(c)`
  (`digs.rs:242` to 246). Only a chunk with no ring-1 brick waits out a `DIG_FADE` fade.
- **The slot.** `open_now` (`digs.rs:392`) opens ring 1 into a free slot in the edit's step, and
  withdraws the last open non-dig job where none is free. A withdrawn job with rounds in flight
  keeps its slot until they are read back (`JobLoop::withdraw`, `bake/job_loop.rs:213` to 217).
- **The submit.** One dispatch a step, while fewer than `IN_FLIGHT` (2) are unread
  (`step.rs:382`); ring 1 is promoted, so `plan_rounds` (`job_loop.rs:397`) gives it the cap
  first. **The cap** is `open_step`'s (`step.rs:223` to 228):
  `(slice + edit_ms while a dig waits or bakes) * 1e6 / ns_per_sample`, clamped to 16 384 and
  `SUBMIT_SAMPLES`. `ns_per_sample` is `0.8 * old + 0.2 * (gpu ms / samples)` of the submits
  read back in a step with at least 4096 samples (`step.rs:417` to 422).
- **The read-back.** `map_async` at the submit (`job_loop.rs:595`); the step's `Poll`
  (`step.rs:272`) and `collect` with `unread = IN_FLIGHT` take what is mapped and wait for
  nothing. The block is placed by the sink in that `collect`.

- The dig's mechanism (Plan 0098 Phases 16 to 18) works as written. No fix to the jump, the
  withdrawal, the slots or the fade's wait.
- **The cap's pricing is a defect**: a mean cost a sample, taken over submits of one size, is
  used to size a submit of another. It bites where a dispatch's fixed cost is near the slice
  (the RENOIR under its line) and the slice then grows (a dig). In reverse it overdraws: the
  first steps after the view drains are sized at the drain's mean (6.0 ms steady gives about
  14 ns; a 1.0 ms slice then sends about 71 000 samples, about 1.7 ms by the fit).
- The nfr's sentence is a reading that did not cover a drained RENOIR. With honest pricing the
  RENOIR's 4.0 ms buys `(4.0 - 0.8) / 12 ns`, about 260 000 samples, to 330 000 at the waiting
  fit's 9.7 ns: dig 1 and dig 2's rings fit, dig 3's 275 022 is at the edge. Expected after the
  fix: 1, 2, 1 or 1, 3, 2. That is the owner's question 7, not a defect.
- Not this plan's, to the backlog: under the RENOIR's 1.0 ms line about four fifths of the
  slice is the dispatch's fixed part.

fixed part is where a line of that slope through the recent read-backs (0.8 a read-back) meets

yet the price is the mean (fixed 0). Under a steady slice the cap draws what the mean draws

this keeps the ns (the slope) and re-reads the fixed part: the small submits read the level

### 1. The estimate as built (code, `bake/budget.rs`)

- `read`: the mean as before; the decayed sums `recent` (dispatches, samples, ms; 0.8 a
  read-back); the fit's span of 64. A fit is taken at a spread of 1.25, or of the last taken
  fit's spread up to 3, and kept only if its slope is over 0 and its standard error within 10 %.
  A rejected fit changes nothing: the negative fit of attempt 1 cannot reset anything here.
- `price`: with a slope, `fixed = (ms - slope x samples) / dispatches` over `recent`; over 0
  that is the price, else `(0, ms / samples)` of `recent`; with no slope `(0, mean)`.
- `cap`: `(ms - fixed) / per`, clamped to 16 384 and `SUBMIT_SAMPLES`. A fixed part over the
  slice gives the floor.

**Derived.** At a steady read rate `recent` holds 5 dispatches' weight, so one read-back slower
than the line by `e` ms raises the fixed part by `e / 5` and the excess decays 0.8 a read-back
(a third left after five). An outlier that pushes the fixed part over the slice sends the floor;
the mean's rule under the same outlier (20 000 samples in 20 ms: `0.8 x 50 + 0.2 x 1000` =
240 ns, 16 600 samples for 4.0 ms) sends about the same, so the worst case is not worse. A stale
slope does not move a steady cap (the line passes through what was drawn); it moves a changed
slice's cap by the slope's own error.

### 6. Why the figures are all in (derived)

A class's ns net of the fixed part needs the fixed part that held when it was read. The general
price's reads 0.000 to 0.47 ms through quiet B and 0.39 to 1.16 ms through A's dig rows; one
ring-1 read-back a dig, read against a fixed part off by 0.8 ms, moves a 4.0 ms cap by a
quarter. All in, a class's figure read at one grant is exact at that grant (the cap it gives
costs the grant: `fixed + per x cap = ms` whatever the split), and a dig's submits all go out
under `slice + edit_ms`. Where the slice changes between a class's read-backs (the view fills
again under a dig) the figure is off by the fixed part's share: a larger grant underdraws, a
smaller one overdraws by at most the fixed part, ruling 2's limit of the Phase 2b ruling. A
three-class fit kept live was not taken: four unknowns over 64 read-backs with a class absent
most of the time.

**The first reading sets the figure** and later ones move it a fifth: with one read-back a dig
and 0.4 ms of spread in a submit's time, a cap that follows the last reading alone would swing
between 183 000 and 276 000 on quiet B's ring-1 submits (4.0 ms over 14.5 to 21.9 ns all in).
