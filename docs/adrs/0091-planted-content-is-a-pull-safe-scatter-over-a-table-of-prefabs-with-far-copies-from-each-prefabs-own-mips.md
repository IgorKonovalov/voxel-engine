# ADR-0091: Planted content is a pull-safe scatter over a table of prefabs, with far copies from each prefab's own mips; the engine's grower is one supplier of prefabs among others

> **Status:** proposed (draft, drafted on Opus 2026-11-28; a Fable second pass reviews it with
> Plan 0116)
> **Date:** 2026-11-28
> **Related plan(s):** [Plan 0116](../plans/0116-planted-content-a-spaced-scatter-of-any-prefab-grown-trees-roots-and-logs-and-their-far-copies.md)

## Context

Trees today are content inside one source: `apps/flythrough/src/trees.rs` keeps one tree per
48-voxel cell by a hash, builds a trunk and a hash-thinned crown sphere, and stamps it from
inside `OutdoorSource`'s `chunk`. Every consumer that wants trees must solve the same three
things again: where a tree stands, without regard to chunk order (a crown crosses chunk
edges); spacing (a per-cell hash clumps and cannot layer shrubs around trunks); and what a
tree is in the far field (ADR-0028's clip chunks are generated by the source directly, and
Plan 0033's answer for the classic trees is a special canopy-envelope rule).

The existing mechanisms do not reach. Plan 0113's anchors are bounded by a reach of at most 16
voxels, and a tree is 100 or more. ADR-0066's placements are a list the consumer computes up
front, which suits authored buildings, but an unbounded forest cannot be listed.

The owner (2026-11-28) wants trees grown by space colonisation with dense leaf masses (the
backlog's crown sparkle is the hash-thinned crown), in four forms plus roots and fallen logs,
true silhouettes in the far field ("shrunk copies"). He also wants **consumers able to provide
trees themselves**: a tree from a `.vox`, from the consumer's own builder, or placed by hand.

## Decision

`svet-world` gains three separable mechanisms. Placement does not know where a prefab came
from.

1. **A scatter**: candidates on a lattice of cells, one per cell per layer, each with a
   position, a hashed priority and a density test that the consumer supplies through a
   surface callback. A candidate survives when it passes its density test and no candidate
   of the same layer within the layer's radius that also passes has a higher priority (ties
   to the lower cell). It also needs no survivor of an earlier layer within the cross radius.
   The rule is not recursive, so a chunk decides from a bounded neighbourhood of cells, in
   any order, in integer arithmetic.
2. **`PlantedSource<S, P>`**, a source wrapper beside `StampedSource`. It writes, into each
   chunk, every planted prefab whose box meets the chunk, through the stamp's write path. It
   takes plantings from a `Planting` trait. The scatter is the engine's implementation, and
   a consumer's own (a hand-placed list from a level file, its own rule) implements the same
   trait. Items are a table of `Arc<Prefab>` with an anchor and a weight. **Any prefab
   qualifies.**
3. **Far copies from the prefab**: each item's prefab mips at levels 1 to 3 are built once,
   at registration, under the far field's `MipRule`. `PlantedSource::coarse_chunk` writes them
   at the planting's position snapped to the level's cell, over the inner source's coarse
   answer. The scatter is the same function at every distance, so a tree in the far field is
   the same tree, shrunk.

A seeded **grower** (space colonisation, Runions, Lane & Prusinkiewicz 2007, in fixed point,
with branch radii by the pipe model and Plan 0115's blob builder at the tips) is one way to
make prefabs for that table. It takes an envelope trait the consumer can implement, and it
names no plant.

## Consequences

### Positive
- A consumer gets spacing, chunk-crossing and far copies for its own trees, rocks, ruins or
  props by handing over prefabs, with no engine-grown content required.
- The far forest keeps true silhouettes and needs no canopy special case for planted content.
  Plan 0033's envelope rule stays for the classic `trees.rs` trees only.
- `PlantedSource` composes with Plan 0113: `DecoratedSource<PlantedSource<terrain>>` mosses
  logs and branches like any stone.

### Negative
- Generation cost: each chunk evaluates the scatter over its neighbourhood (the candidates
  whose largest item reaches it, and their neighbours within the radius). That is cheap per
  column but paid at all four levels, and coarse chunks cover 8 to 512 times the area.
  Plan 0116 reads it.
- Far copies snap to the level's cell, so a tree moves by up to half a cell (up to 40 cm at
  level 3) at a level switch. Plan 0033's dithered band is what hides it, and it is read
  rather than assumed.
- The non-recursive survival rule keeps fewer points than a maximal Poisson-disk set: a
  candidate killed by a neighbour that is itself killed leaves a gap. Density is tuned
  against that, not computed.
- One candidate per cell per layer means one surface per column per layer. A consumer with
  stacked ledges picks which surface a candidate uses, or adds a layer.
- The grower's load cost grows with variants. It runs at load, on threads, and is read
  against the start-up readings.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A: placements as ADR-0066's list
The consumer computes every tree up front and stamps them as placements. This is right for
authored buildings, and it stays for them. It loses for planted content because an unbounded
world cannot be listed, and the list grows with exploration.

### Alternative B: trees as Plan 0113 anchors
Anchors are pull-safe because their prefabs fit the reach of 16 voxels. A tree does not, and
widening the reach to 100 voxels makes a 64-voxel chunk's halo 264³, about 70 times the
chunk's voxels, read per chunk. This was rejected on cost.

### Alternative C: a canopy-density field for the far field
A coarse cell is leaf where crowns cover most of it (Plan 0033's answer for the classic
trees). It is cheaper, but the far forest becomes a blanket with a visible switch to the real
trees at the full-detail edge. The owner picked shrunk copies (2026-11-28).

### Alternative D: L-systems as the grower
Rewriting rules per species, with no competition for space, so crowns need hand-tuned
pruning to avoid self-intersection. They stay available to a consumer through the prefab
table. The engine's grower is colonisation because its crown is just an envelope, and
competition gives natural gaps.
