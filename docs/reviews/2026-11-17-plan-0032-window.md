# Plan 0032's window, 2026-11-17: the diagnosis and the options

> **Written:** 2026-11-17, on the lane `plan-0032` at `dbf7f31d` (stacked on `plan-0069`),
> after the owner's Phase 9 window of the same day. **Read-only:** no cargo, no GPU run (the
> owner's sessions and two lanes share the perf lock). Every figure is read from the session
> logs `live/session32-NN.log`, the plan's readings log (`docs/plans/logs/0032.md`, Phases 5
> to 9) or `docs/nfr/02-frame-budget.md`; every mechanism is read from the shader or source it
> names. A claim this review infers rather than reads says so.
> **Asked for by the owner:** "invoke fable to think about all of this and come with an
> options. shadows looks great but performance lacks, and light flickers". The verdict
> verbatim and the screenshots' descriptions are in the readings log's Phase 9 block.
> **What this is not:** a plan amendment. The owner picks from the options first; the
> architect then amends Plan 0032 (Phase 10 and a fix pass) and writes the ADR the pick needs.

The one-paragraph verdict: the sky, the sunset and the shadows' geometry are right, and
every complaint but the sun's size traces to **two rules of the plan's own design** and one
property of the lighting base. First, "an absent chunk inside the residency ball is solid"
(`walk.wgsl:678`, Decision 2) shadows the far ground for the 7 to 10 s the world takes to
stream after the first frame and at the leading edge of every move, which is the far-ground
flip and most of "the light settles". Second, the key light's shadow ray and the sky ray are
each **one binary sample on an eleven-frame blend** (`sky_light.wgsl`, `temporal.wgsl`
`ALPHA` 0.2), and the blend's history is rejected wherever the voxel staircase's tread and
riser alternate under FSR 2's jitter (`temporal.wgsl`'s `HISTORY_COS` test), so a raw 0-or-full
sample shows along every stair contour: the tunnel's sparkle, the forest floor's speckle bands
and the penumbra's grain are the same estimator. Third, the canopy at fill 128 leaves about
0.4 % of its 2^3 blocks empty to the coarse walk, so every tree casts sun flecks the eye reads
as noise; the owner's own words ask for the canopy to shadow as a solid. Performance on the
RTX is not the sky (about 1 to 1.5 ms of a 10 ms window frame): the owner felt the **CPU**
settle, where the bake queue's step costs 8 to 15 ms a frame on a world with no emitter, and
the forest's sync spikes. On the RADV iGPU the sky's two rays cost 9.7 ms of a 45.7 ms frame.

## The diagnosis, per problem

### B. The far ground flips between lit and shadowed; "lighted color as a default"

- **The rule.** `sky_shadowed` sets `absent_ball_r2 = sky.ball_r2` around its walk
  (`sky_light.wgsl:33`); the walk returns a hit at the first chunk whose table entry is
  `ABSENT` within that squared chunk distance of the camera's chunk (`walk.wgsl:678-684`).
  The ball is `max(r_full, r_lod)^2 = 24^2` chunks (`frame/settings.rs:440`), the whole
  residency sphere: at full residency no chunk inside it is absent (step 1: 17 077 full plus
  40 700 LOD-only chunks, a sphere of radius 24), so the rule bites only while chunks are
  pending.
- **When chunks are pending.** Full residency comes 7 to 10 s after the first frame in every
  step that reached it (step 1: first frame at 6.7 s, full residency at 14.0 s; step 11:
  10.5 and 18.3 s; readings log, Phase 9). The stream fills near to far, so the shell is last,
  and a sun ray from ground 20 chunks out rises through the shell before it leaves the ball.
  While the camera moves, the leading edge of the sphere is always partly absent: the ground
  ahead reads shadowed, then lit as the chunks arrive, then shadowed again at the next move.
  That is the flip the owner saw, and "long time for light to settle" (steps 4 and 11) is the
  same 7 to 10 s.
- **Not the cause.** The LOD level: every LOD-only chunk inside the ball is walked at level 1
  (`lod1_r2 = (2 r_full)^2 = 32^2`, `frame/encode.rs:217`), so no mip level changes inside
  the ball as the camera moves. The hills' real self-shadow at a 45-degree noon sun (slopes
  up to about 67 degrees from the noise's amplitudes) is part of what Image #3 shows on the
  north faces, and that part is correct and still.
- **What the owner asks for** is the other default: where the shadow ray cannot be resolved,
  light. Plan 0032 Decision 2 and ADR-0027 chose "unknown is not sky" so that a chunk not yet
  streamed does not let the sun into a cave. The leak the rule prevents is a transient at the
  ball's edge, and a ray that *leaves* the ball is already sky, so the rule's protection is
  thin: it trades a transient leak for a transient shadow over the whole far field.

### C. The tunnel's edge and far end flicker

- **The estimator.** At each lighting pixel with a hit, one cosine ray about the normal; the
  sample is `pi sky_radiance(d)` if it reaches the sky within 512 voxels and 0 otherwise
  (`sky_light.wgsl:76-91`), with no clamp, added to the direct target pre-exposed
  (`shade.wgsl:104-118`). The direct accumulation blends it at `max(0.2, 1 / (age + 1))`
  (`temporal.wgsl:365, 553`), eleven frames to 90 %.
- **Inside the tunnel** the ray reaches the sky only through the mouth. Near the mouth the
  hit rate is intermediate (a Bernoulli at p about 0.3 to 0.6 on a 0.2 blend has a standard
  deviation of about a sixth of the full value: the "flicker on tunnel edge"). Toward the far
  end the mouth subtends a few percent of the hemisphere: a rare hit adds the whole `pi L`,
  thirty times the mean, and decays over three or four frames: the "super flickery"
  sparkling disc of Image #5. The auto exposure, adapted to the dark interior, scales both.
- **The plan's guard did not see it.** Phase 6's stop rule compared the sky ray's stillness
  with the lamp's on an open floor under a uniform sky, where every ray reaches the sky and
  the variance is exactly 0 (readings log, Phase 6: "the estimate is pi at every sample"). The
  rig had no partial occluder, so the rule passed at `N` = 1 without reading the case the
  window shows. Whatever option is picked below, its first phase is a reading of the sky
  term alone at the tunnel pose, so the fix is attributed and not guessed.
- **The exit's white** (Image #4) is the eye model with no highlight roll-off past ACES: a
  +10 EV opening against an interior-adapted exposure. The owner called the entrance fine;
  no option is offered for it.

### D. Tree shadows sparkle while the light settles and under a fixed sun

Three mechanisms stack, read in this order of weight (the first two are read from the code
and the images, the third from the arithmetic):

1. **The same binary estimator at the stair contours.** The speckles in Images #6 and #7
   follow the terrain's contour steps in arcs and bands. A lighting pixel at a stair edge
   lands on the tread (normal +z) one frame and the riser (+y) the next under FSR 2's jitter;
   the temporal pass rejects history when `dot(prev.n, rec.n) < HISTORY_COS` or the material
   differs (`temporal.wgsl:488-489`, `HISTORY_COS` 0.8), and a rejected pixel takes this frame's sample as it is
   (`a = 1`). In the shade that sample is the sky ray's 0-or-full; in the penumbra it is the
   key light's 0-or-full. The a-trous filters the direct part (`atrous.wgsl:116-118`) but its
   luminance weight is driven by the moments' variance, which is 0 at a fresh pixel (the
   temporal pass takes `a_moments = 1` there), so the filter leaves those pixels alone.
   "When light settles" (step 11) adds mechanism B's flips on top.
2. **Sun flecks through the canopy.** The coarse shadow walk treats a 2^3 block with any set
   bit as solid; a canopy voxel is kept with probability 128/256, so a block is empty with
   probability `0.5^8 = 0.0039`. A canopy of radius 23.5 voxels holds about 6800 blocks, so
   about 27 of them are holes, each a 2-voxel sun fleck on the ground that moves with the sun
   and the jitter. At fill 64 (`0.75^8 = 0.10`) a tenth of the blocks are holes: dappled
   light, which the owner liked for the trees' look but which multiplies the flecks. The
   LOD ring's trees are mip-1 cells under the same any-rule at every block, which is why
   "trees are coarse, shadows look amazing": the far canopy is a solid blob.
3. **The penumbra's grain at 3 degrees.** The pick widens every penumbra: a canopy 80
   voxels up casts a penumbra `2 x 80 x tan 3 = 8.4` voxels wide, and a single disc ray on a
   0.2 blend is noisiest at its middle (standard deviation 0.17 of the full value at p 0.5).
   The soft edge the owner liked at step 5 is a wide band of that noise; it reads soft from
   the flat ground at 16:00 and will read as grain on the forest floor.

### E. Performance

- **The RTX at the window** (FSR 2 at 1.5 from about 1688 x 920): the frame's GPU reads 9.2
  to 10.7 ms in every step but the full tier (18.3 ms); sky rays 1, 2 and 4 read 10.73, 10.06
  and 10.51 ms, inside the run-to-run spread. Headless at 1080p (readings log, Phase 7) the
  noon frame is 5.6 to 5.9 ms against 4.4 to 4.9 with the sky off, so the sky (the LUTs 0.1
  ms, the key ray and the sky ray about 0.25 ms each at 1080p, the rest the sky's longer light
  pass over far hits) is 1 to 1.5 ms of the window's 10. The remaining 8 to 9 ms are the hit
  pass over the outdoor scene, FSR 2's passes (2.1 to 2.4 ms on the RTX at this output,
  `docs/nfr/02-frame-budget.md`) and the a-trous.
- **The CPU settle is what "performance lacks" felt like on the RTX.** The runs the owner
  cut short read a frame CPU p50 of 10 to 17.6 ms (steps 6, 7, 8, 10, 14, 15, 16) against 1.3
  to 2.9 ms in the long runs, and the `bake queue:` lines read `step CPU p50` 7.9 to 15.0 ms,
  max up to 38 ms, for the 7 to 10 s of the settle, on a world whose reach holds `lights 0`
  and whose store ends all zeros. After the queue drains the step is 0.05 ms. The forest at
  fill 128 (step 15) also read a sync p99 of 11.0 ms (alloc 5.3) and `update + poll + sync`
  max 150 ms; fill 64 read 3.1 ms. Neither is this plan's mechanism; both are readings for
  Plan 0067's rule (the queue's step) and the sync's backlog entry.
- **The RADV RENOIR** (headless 1080p, Phase 7): noon 45.7 ms against 36.0 with the sky off
  and 22.9 in the cave; the light pass 10.5 against 1.2 ms. Phase 6's table reads the sky
  ray at about 4.8 ms a ray there (1 to 2 rays: 10.5 to 15.3 ms), so the key ray is about 4.5
  ms and the sky ray about 4.8 ms. The forest at 10 degrees adds 1.2 to 1.9 ms of light pass
  over the open hills; the full walk 2.6 ms more (Phase 8's table). The far horizon's primary
  rays, not the sky, make the iGPU's outdoor frame 13 ms dearer than its cave frame.

## The options

Each problem: two or three concrete options, what each changes, cost and benefit, the risk to
the stable image, and the recommended first. "ADR" marks an option that reverses a recorded
decision and so needs one. The mechanism phases of every C and D option start with a reading
that splits the defect by term (key light, sky ray, both, filter off and on) at the tunnel and
forest poses, since Phase 6's rig did not.
